# Editorial Style Sheet

## Authority and scope

This sheet governs all archetype and shadow manuscripts, front and back matter, profile panels, navigation labels, and editorial ledgers. The canonical names, slugs, lifecycle placements, quality pairs, and relationships in `canonical-taxonomy.md` take precedence over legacy copy and generated JSON.

Use Merriam-Webster for general American spelling and *The Chicago Manual of Style* for questions not settled here. Record deliberate exceptions in this file before applying them globally.

## Voice

Write in a voice that is:

- authoritative but warm;
- direct, concise, and psychologically observant;
- archetypal without making decorative mystical claims;
- instructive without lecturing;
- compassionate without excusing impact;
- able to hold paradox without reducing it to a slogan; and
- occasionally poetic only when the image carries a precise psychological insight.

Prefer concrete behavior, consequence, choice, and repair over abstract uplift. State the mechanism before offering metaphor. Do not diagnose the reader or infer a wound, motive, trauma, or disorder as fact. When a protective history is relevant, use conditional language: “She may have learned…” or “The pattern can protect…”

Do not use promotional language, inflated significance, decorative mysticism, generic inspiration, throat clearing, synonym cycling, forced rules of three, or automatic “not X, but Y” constructions. Never pad an entry to fill its spread. Expansion must add a new distinction, application, tension, example, practice, or insight.

## Point of view and address

- Use **she/her** for an archetype or personified shadow: “The Guardian protects what is vulnerable. She also knows when to stand down.”
- Use **we/our** for shared human practices and invitations: “We can test trust in graduated steps.”
- Reserve **you/your** mainly for explicit reflection questions. Do not use second person to accuse, diagnose, or announce what the reader secretly wants.
- Use **they/them** for a person whose gender is unknown or irrelevant inside an example.
- Keep the primary reader—a woman exploring mature feminine development—visible without claiming that all women share a nature, role, body, history, or path.

## Lifecycle language

The lifecycle is **Maiden → Lover → Mother → Queen → Crone**. Capitalize these words when they name the five taxonomic stages or the canonical Maiden, Mother, Queen, and Crone archetypes. Lowercase them in ordinary descriptive use when no taxonomic meaning is intended.

The stages are revisitable psychological territories. Never present them as:

- fixed ages or a one-way maturity ladder;
- reproductive states or biological requirements;
- relationship or marital statuses;
- a universal sequence every woman completes; or
- ranks in which a later stage is inherently more valuable.

“Young” in Young Visionary and Young Healer names early formation in that function. “Elder” in Elder Healer and Elder Visionary names experience with limits, consequence, and stewardship. Neither word asserts chronological age. Mother names the capacity to sustain development; it does not require pregnancy or parenting. Lover and Devoted Consort do not require sex, romance, marriage, or partnership.

## Canonical names, aliases, and capitalization

- Use exactly one canonical title from `canonical-taxonomy.md` in the H1, running head, table of contents, navigation, URL, profile panel, and declaration lead-in.
- Do not use slash-separated titles, parenthetical deity names, stacked aliases, or deity epithets in headings.
- Alternate functional names such as Creatrix, Huntress, Oracle, Hearth Keeper, Love Goddess, Priestess, Alchemist, Witch, or Scribe may appear sparingly in body prose only when the alternate clarifies an expression that the canonical name alone does not.
- Capitalize a canonical archetype or shadow when personified as the named pattern: “the Mature Creator,” “the Compulsive Perfecter.” Lowercase a generic role: “a creator,” “a perfectionist habit.”
- Capitalize **Active Shadow** and **Passive Shadow** in profile labels and designation lines. Use lowercase in ordinary prose: “the active shadow inflates daring.”
- Capitalize the two canonical qualities when they appear as profile labels or section terms. Lowercase them in ordinary syntax unless personified.
- Use **Mature** before an archetype only in a declaration heading or where contrast with a shadow requires it. Maturity is the balanced practice, not a superior identity.
- Slugs are lowercase kebab case and never include an alias, deity, punctuation mark, or lifecycle word already supplied by the route.

## Headings and entry architecture

Each independently renderable source has one H1 only: the canonical title. Use H2 for major teaching sections and H3 only when a genuine subsection is needed. Do not skip heading levels. Headings use title case, no terminal punctuation, and no decorative numbering.

Required functions must be present, but section names may be tailored so the book does not read like a repeated template.

An archetype spread must include:

1. one verified opening quotation;
2. image and compact profile panel;
3. a strong definition of the mature expression;
4. eight to twelve declarations;
5. the two-quality balance and each one-sided failure;
6. relevant application in inner life, relationship, work, community, or creation;
7. distinctive gifts, responsibilities, and challenges;
8. concrete embodiment or integration practices;
9. a `Living as the [Archetype]` conclusion; and
10. one verified closing quotation.

A shadow spread must include:

1. one canonical H1 and an Active Shadow or Passive Shadow designation;
2. one verified opening quotation;
3. image and compact profile panel;
4. the exact breakdown of the parent's balance;
5. six to nine declarations;
6. psychological mechanics and possible protective purpose;
7. the useful energy hidden inside the shadow;
8. recognition in concrete domains where relevant;
9. practices that restore the missing quality;
10. a clear relation to the opposite shadow;
11. transformation toward the parent;
12. a grounded conclusion; and
13. one verified closing quotation.

Avoid generic headings such as “In Daily Life” when a precise heading can name the entry's actual tension. Do not turn every paragraph into a heading or repeat the title in adjacent headings.

## Balance and shadow language

State the balance equation exactly once in the profile and explain it in prose:

`Mature [Archetype] = [Quality A] + [Quality B]`

The active shadow is Quality A inflated, weaponized, or overexpressed without Quality B. The passive shadow is the collapse or abdication of Quality A while Quality B becomes a refuge, distortion, or substitute. Show the causal chain in observable behavior; do not merely list two vices.

Integration is not suppression of the shadow's energy. It restores the missing quality and the relationship between both qualities. Name the useful energy the shadow carries, the effect of the imbalance, and a concrete practice of return. Never call a shadow evil, toxic, crazy, broken, narcissistic, hysterical, or a fixed personality type.

## Declarations

Declarations are first-person present-tense lines that reveal the mature stance or shadow logic from inside. They must be specific enough that another archetype could not plausibly claim the same set.

- Archetypes receive eight to twelve declarations; shadows receive six to nine.
- Use a bulleted list. End every full-sentence declaration with a period.
- Prefer one independent clause or two tightly related clauses. Avoid semicolon chains and mini-essays.
- Keep parallel grammar where it sharpens rhythm, but do not force every line into the same template.
- Do not use quotation marks around declarations.
- Avoid generic claims such as “I trust the journey,” “I choose growth,” or “I stand in my power.”
- Do not recycle a declaration, lightly paraphrase it for another entry, or make all active shadows controlling and all passive shadows afraid.

## Quotations and attribution

Every final archetype and shadow has exactly two short, verified quotations: one opening and one closing. Quote wording, attribution, and context must match the source ledger before layout.

- Use curly double quotation marks for a quotation and curly single quotation marks for a quotation within it.
- Follow American punctuation: periods and commas go inside closing quotation marks; colons and semicolons go outside; question and exclamation marks follow meaning.
- Preserve the source's spelling and meaningful punctuation. Mark an omission with a spaced ellipsis (` … `) and a supplied or altered word with square brackets. Do not silently “improve” source language.
- Put attribution on its own line: `—Author, *Work*`. The em dash is tight to the author name.
- Italicize book, long poem, film, album, journal, and standalone-work titles. Put article, chapter, essay, short poem, and interview titles in quotation marks.
- Do not append an unattributed web slogan, paraphrase, translation of unknown provenance, or quotation commonly attributed without primary/reputable support.
- Prefer women writers, psychologists, poets, mystics, elders, artists, and cultural figures. Precision outranks demographic quota when an exceptional alternative is demonstrably stronger.
- Keep quotations short enough for the spread and conservative enough for quotation-rights review.

## Punctuation, grammar, and numbers

- Use the serial, or **Oxford, comma** in a series of three or more.
- Use **tight em dashes**—with no spaces—for a true interruption or amplification. Prefer a period, comma, colon, or parentheses when it does the job. Do not use em dashes as automatic rhythm.
- Use an en dash for ranges and paired spans: `8–12 declarations`, `2025–2026`, `Maiden–Crone distinction`. Do not substitute a hyphen.
- Use one space after terminal punctuation.
- Prefer active voice when agency matters. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or deliberately de-centered.
- Avoid exclamation marks outside a verified quotation. Do not use rhetorical questions as transitions.
- Spell out zero through nine in running prose; use numerals for 10 and above. Use numerals for percentages, dimensions, page numbers, dates, times, measurements, and exact counts in technical or production text.
- At the start of a sentence, spell out a number or rewrite the sentence. Use commas in numbers of 1,000 or more.
- Write decades as `the 1990s`, not `the 1990's`. Write percentages as `15%` in technical text and `15 percent` in literary body prose when the number is not a measured result.
- Use `for example` rather than `e.g.` and `that is` rather than `i.e.` in reader-facing prose.

## Mythic resonances

Mythic figures appear only in the compact **Mythic Resonances** profile field and, when indispensable, in one restrained body reference that clarifies a psychological function. They do not appear in titles or explanatory mythology sections.

- Verify every resonance in the source ledger with the tradition, function claimed, spelling/transliteration, and a reputable source.
- Label traditions precisely. Do not call a figure merely “ancient,” “Eastern,” “Indigenous,” or “goddess tradition.”
- Never present figures from different traditions as names for the same universal being or collapse their differences into a type.
- Do not use a deity or cultural figure as decoration, exotic atmosphere, or proof of a universal claim.
- Distinguish deity, spirit, saint, literary character, historical person, and ritual office.
- Indigenous and living religious traditions require heightened cultural-context review. Remove an association when appropriate sourcing or context is unavailable.
- A resonance list is not evidence for the archetype's psychological claims. The function must stand without mythology.

## Inclusive and psychologically responsible language

- Describe patterns and actions, not fixed kinds of women.
- Distinguish impact from intent and context from excuse.
- Avoid biological essentialism, heteronormativity, compulsory motherhood, age hierarchy, racialized tropes, class assumptions, and spiritual superiority.
- Do not prescribe reconciliation where contact is unsafe or accountability absent.
- Do not present boundaries as punishment, forgiveness as obligation, suffering as spiritual failure, or professional care as unnecessary.
- Use examples across inner life, intimate and non-intimate relationships, work, community, and creation only when relevant to the function.

## Editing sequence

The passes occur in this order. A later pass may send a documented problem back to an earlier pass, but it may not silently reopen settled work.

### 1. Developmental edit

Test the function, lifecycle placement, quality pair, parent-shadow causality, opposite-shadow polarity, integration path, applications, practices, and distinctness from every neighbor. Cut or move material that belongs to another entry. Confirm that every required spread function is present. Do not polish sentences whose conceptual job is still unstable.

Gate: the entry teaches one necessary function, both shadows visibly derive from its balance, and no section duplicates another entry.

### 2. Anti-fluff and AI-pattern edit

Delete repeated ideas, generic uplift, decorative mysticism, inflated claims, throat clearing, vague transitions, forced tricolons, synonym cycling, canned empathy, empty paradox, excessive hedging, and formulaic “This is not X; it is Y” prose. Compare declarations and practices across the whole book for recycled language. Expansion is allowed only for a named missing distinction.

Gate: every paragraph adds new teaching, every example is concrete, and every sentence sounds specific to this entry.

### 3. Line edit

Improve clarity, force, rhythm, paragraph sequence, transition logic, image precision, declaration parallelism, and sentence variety. Resolve ambiguous pronouns and abstract nouns. Read aloud. Preserve the approved voice and conceptual structure rather than redesigning them.

Gate: the prose can be read once without confusion, and rhythm serves meaning rather than calling attention to itself.

### 4. Copy edit

Make restrained, mechanical corrections to grammar, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, number style, quotation treatment, headings, metadata, cross-references, canonical names, and profile consistency. Check every quote and mythic-source identifier against its ledger. Do not reopen voice, add examples, or rewrite for preference during this pass.

Gate: the manuscript conforms to this sheet, all names and relationships match the canonical taxonomy, and no factual or source placeholder remains.

## Production fit is not an editorial pass

Exact-spread fitting follows the four edits. When an entry runs long, cut redundancy before unique teaching. When it runs short, return to the developmental record and add only an identified missing distinction, application, tension, example, practice, or insight. Never add filler, duplicate declarations, shrink one entry to tiny type, or weaken a quality distinction merely to make pagination pass.
