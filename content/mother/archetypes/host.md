# Host

“[T]he mere foothold of a house, easily accessible, ample in space, hospitable and tolerant in spirit … would be in itself a serviceable thing for Chicago.”

—Jane Addams, *Twenty Years at Hull-House*, chapter V, “First Days at Hull-House”

## A Welcome With a Door

The Host maintains bounded hospitality and a usable sanctuary. She shapes space, time, attention, food, information, and rhythm so people can settle and meet. Her place may be a home, classroom, clinic, community table, digital gathering, shelter, or inner refuge. Welcome has shape without depending on décor or ownership.

Her hearth has a door. Privacy protects rest, intimacy, concentration, history, and what is not available for communal use. She opens generously and closes clearly. A guest is not entitled to every room, hour, or truth; a host need not disappear into service.

Hosting is a human function, not feminized destiny or the property of wives, mothers, homeowners, or one household form. The Mother develops capacity, the Guardian decides safe access, and the Community Weaver builds reciprocal ties. The Host's craft is the living threshold between sanctuary and encounter.

## The Host's Inner Stance

- I make welcome specific enough that everyone can know its shape.
- I protect privacy without making relationship an intrusion.
- I offer what I can sustain rather than what proves devotion.
- I let guests retain choice, dignity, and a path to leave.
- I name household labor before it becomes invisible obligation.
- I do not admit unsafe access in the name of generosity.
- I keep some time, space, and attention unavailable for renewal.
- I adapt a place to bodies and needs different from my own.
- I receive contribution without turning hospitality into a debt.
- I can close the door with warmth and open it without self-erasure.

## The Living Threshold

Hospitality makes a place usable beyond its keeper. It offers orientation, comfort, access, and enough predictability to participate without guessing every rule. It can be simple, shared, temporary, and materially honest.

Privacy protects the conditions from which welcome is possible: bodily boundaries, confidences, solitude, rest, protected spaces, household intimacy, and the right not to host. It is selective access, not secrecy that conceals harm.

Without Privacy, Hospitality consumes the sanctuary and turns sacrifice into resentment or debt. When Hospitality collapses, Privacy becomes an enclosure that excludes ordinary relationship. Hospitality keeps privacy from hardening; privacy keeps hospitality from consuming the household.

## Where Sanctuary Takes Form

A mature place serves the lives within it and the encounters it chooses to hold.

### Inner Life

She creates a reliable transition out of demand: a walk, closed device, meal, prayer, or period without response. This room lets her notice fatigue before every knock feels intrusive.

### Household and Relationship

She states expectations for time, keys, expenses, noise, accessibility, and private areas. No woman, elder, partner, or competent person automatically absorbs the labor. Everyone whose space and safety are affected gives consent.

### Work and Community

She orients newcomers, explains norms, makes participation accessible, and routes concerns. At scale, hospitality requires budgets, staffing, translation, disability access, safeguarding, cleaning, privacy, and rest. Public welcome requires a crossable material threshold.

### Stewardship and Crisis

She offers temporary shelter or urgent care with named duration, capacity, risk, and handoff. Housing precarity, migration, abuse, illness, poverty, and disaster are material conditions. Refusing unsafe private burden is not moral failure; institutions must carry their part.

## What the Hearth Asks of Her

The Host carries attentiveness, practical imagination, and respect for rhythms that restore steadiness. Available food, findable information, and repeated tasks can make strangers less disoriented and households more habitable. She distinguishes preference from necessary rule, notices whose labor or displacement makes the place possible, and rejects welcome built on underpayment, inaccessibility, assimilation, or exhaustion.

She accepts that boundaries disappoint. She may refuse a stay, end a gathering, protect a housemate's privacy, or bar unsafe conduct. Hospitality never requires admission amid coercion, abuse, stalking, or credible threat.

## Practices of Bounded Hospitality

- We state the invitation plainly: who is included, what is offered, when it begins and ends, and which parts of the space remain private.
- We ask everyone affected about safety, access, quiet, substances, food, caregiving, animals, and material needs.
- We list and assign setup, hosting, cleaning, transport, and follow-up labor before the gathering.
- We reserve one period, room, device setting, or ritual for privacy and protect it before our capacity is gone.
- We orient a newcomer without requiring personal disclosure, social fluency, gratitude, or immediate intimacy.
- We offer a contribution as optional and receive another person's contribution without keeping a moral score.
- We make an ending hospitable by giving notice, helping with transition, and keeping the stated departure time.
- We name unsafe conduct and restrict access according to evidence, role, law, and the needs of those carrying risk.
- We review material sustainability and recruit shared, institutional, or professional support when private capacity is insufficient.

## Living as the Host

Living as the Host means tending a place that can breathe. Hospitality lets relationship cross the threshold; Privacy keeps interior life intact. Warmth permits arrival, clarity participation, boundaries safety, and closure sanctuary for everyone who lives there, including her.
